# Go Below — image generation prompts

Eleven images. Generated rather than stock, because the thing we are selling —
a *bespoke luxury* underground space with daylight in it — barely exists in
stock libraries. What you will find there is either a municipal car park, a
wine cellar, or a military bunker. None of those is the brand.

## The one rule that makes or breaks every image

Each image must be **obviously underground** and **obviously desirable at the
same time**. The way you prove "underground" is *not* by making it dark. It is
by showing:

- light arriving **only from above**, never from a window in a wall
- **thick** structure — deep reveals, heavy soffits, visible mass
- **earth-retaining** walls: board-marked or shuttered concrete
- ground level visible *above* you through the opening — grass, planting, sky

If a viewer could mistake the room for a nice ground-floor extension, the
image has failed. If it looks like a shelter, it has failed the other way.

## Shared style suffix

Append this to every prompt below:

> architectural photography, shot on a 24mm tilt-shift lens, natural light,
> editorial interiors quality, calm and still, no people, no text, no logos,
> photorealistic, high dynamic range, warm neutral colour grade

## Shared negative prompt

> fisheye, wide-angle distortion, HDR halos, teal and orange grade, blue
> cinematic tint, neon, sci-fi, video game render, cluttered, busy, cartoon,
> illustration, people, faces, text, watermark, military, tactical, survivalist,
> apocalyptic, grimy, damp, mouldy

## PRIORITY 1 — the hero

Get this one right before anything else. It carries the entire proposition.

### 1. `hero-living-lightwell` — daylight

> A luxurious underground living room directly beneath a large circular
> planted light well. Daylight pours down from a glazed opening far above,
> striking a shallow reflecting pool set into a limestone floor and throwing
> rippling light across board-marked concrete walls. Ferns and trailing
> greenery spill over the rim of the opening high overhead. Low oak and bouclé
> seating, a bronze side table, one large olive tree reaching toward the light.
> The walls are clearly thick, earth-retaining concrete with deep reveals.
> There are no conventional windows anywhere — every scrap of light comes from
> above. Warm, serene, expensive.

**Aspect:** 16:9 · **Must read as:** *"That's underground?"*

## PRIORITY 2 — the six categories

### 2. `living-bedroom-skylight` — daylight

> A calm underground principal bedroom lit by a large rectangular skylight
> directly above the bed. Soft morning daylight falls through the opening onto
> linen bedding and a wide oak headboard. Planting grows at the edge of the
> skylight opening above, visible from below. Walls are warm limestone and
> board-marked concrete, unusually thick, with a deep reveal around the
> opening. No windows in any wall. Restful, residential, not a hotel.

**Aspect:** 4:3

### 3. `wellness-pool-spa` — daylight

> An underground swimming pool and spa. Still turquoise-free water, honest and
> clear, reflecting a run of linear roof lights overhead. Travertine and warm
> stone surrounds, a cedar sauna with a glazed front set into the far wall,
> steam catching the shafts of daylight. Planting at the pool's edge beneath
> the roof lights. Massive concrete structure visible in the deep ceiling
> coffers. Resort-quality, private, no windows in the walls.

**Aspect:** 4:3

### 4. `entertainment-cinema` — deep *(this one should be dark)*

> A private underground cinema. Tiered rows of deep leather recliners facing a
> large screen. Warm low-level lighting concealed in the step nosings and a
> bronze reveal around the screen wall. Acoustic timber slat walls in dark
> oak, heavy wool carpet. The room is genuinely dark because that is its
> purpose — but the materials are rich and the edges are precise, not black on
> black. A glimpse of thick concrete structure where the ceiling meets the
> wall.

**Aspect:** 4:3

### 5. `automotive-vehicle-vault` — warm

> A private underground vehicle vault beneath a country house. Three or four
> classic and modern cars on a mirror-polished resin floor, arranged with
> space around them like sculpture. Structural concrete columns with sharp
> shutter lines, warm linear lighting picking out the paintwork, a glass-walled
> workshop bay at the rear. Absolutely not a public car park — no bay numbers,
> no line markings, no signage, no ramps. Private, collected, expensive.

**Aspect:** 4:3

### 6. `protection-blast-door` — deep

> A heavy engineered security door, closed, set into the end of a *finished
> architectural corridor* — not a military installation. The door is brushed
> stainless steel and bronze with a precision wheel mechanism and visible
> heavy hinges, framed by a deep board-marked concrete reveal. The corridor
> leading to it has warm oak wall panelling, a limestone floor and concealed
> linear lighting. The contrast is the point: domestic luxury leading to
> serious protection. No signage, no hazard markings, no weapons, no khaki.

**Aspect:** 4:5 (portrait)

### 7. `specialist-wine-room` — warm

> An underground wine room. Full-height oak and blackened-steel racking
> holding bottles, a central tasting table in solid oak, cool limestone walls
> and floor. Low warm pooled lighting, deep shadows between the racks. One
> small high slot of daylight entering from above at the far end, just enough
> to say "this is below ground, not a windowless box". Cellar-cool
> atmosphere, precise joinery, no clutter.

**Aspect:** 4:3

## PRIORITY 3 — the descent sequence

These three must feel like **one continuous journey**, so generate them
together and keep the season, weather, palette and house style consistent.

### 8. `descent-concealed-entrance` — daylight

> An immaculate English country garden on a still overcast morning — clipped
> lawn, yew hedging, gravel, a low stone garden wall. Set discreetly into the
> lawn is a single flush glass-and-bronze panel, almost invisible, the only
> hint of anything beneath. No doors, no railings, no signage, no hatch, no
> bunker imagery. The whole point is that you would walk past it. Quiet,
> expensive, English.

**Aspect:** 16:10

### 9. `descent-stair` — warm

> Looking down a wide cantilevered stone staircase descending into the ground.
> Daylight falls from the opening above and fades as the stair descends, met
> from below by a warm concealed strip of light in the handrail. The walls
> thicken visibly as the stair goes down — board-marked concrete, deep
> reveals, a sense of real mass and depth. Bronze balustrade, oak treads over
> stone. The transition from outside to underground made visible.

**Aspect:** 16:10

### 10. `descent-arrival` — daylight

> The view at the bottom of the stair as the space opens out. A tall
> double-height underground hall with a huge roof light overhead, planting
> cascading down one wall, water running in a narrow channel across a
> limestone floor. Board-marked concrete, oak, bronze. Volume and daylight far
> below ground level. The moment the space stops feeling like a basement and
> starts feeling like architecture.

**Aspect:** 16:10

## PRIORITY 4 — engineering credibility

### 11. `engineering-structure` — deep

> A large residential underground excavation during construction, seen from
> ground level looking down. Sheet-piled and propped retaining walls, a dense
> mat of reinforcement steel laid out across the base slab, timber shuttering
> to the wall kickers, a tower crane hook descending. Real scale — the
> excavation is deep enough that the people-sized details read as small. Grey
> British daylight, wet ground, honest construction. Serious civil engineering
> beneath a domestic setting. No people in frame.

**Aspect:** 3:2

## If you would rather shoot or source than generate

Realistically, four of these could be sourced:

| Slot | Where to look |
|---|---|
| `entertainment-cinema` | Home-cinema installers' project galleries — ask permission |
| `specialist-wine-room` | Wine-cellar specialists, same |
| `engineering-structure` | Basement contractors, or a real site visit — best option, it is the one image where authenticity shows |
| `descent-concealed-entrance` | Photographable in any good garden |

The other seven realistically have to be generated until Go Below has built
something photographable. **The day there is a real completed project,
replace the hero first** — a genuine photograph of your own work will
outperform any generated image on this site.

## Technical

- Generate at the **largest size your tool allows**, then supply at 2560px wide
- Save as `public/images/<id>.jpg`
- Set `src: '/images/<id>.jpg'` on that slot in `src/lib/images.ts`
- `next.config.mjs` handles AVIF/WebP conversion and responsive sizing
